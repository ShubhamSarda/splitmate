# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Friend groups on trips and roommates sharing living costs. They're splitting dinner tabs, Airbnbs, groceries, and utilities — not managing a business. They use Splitmate when there's a shared expense to log or a debt to settle. Their context is casual: phone in hand, mid-conversation, slightly distracted. The primary job is "figure out who owes what without making it awkward."

## Product Purpose

Splitmate tracks shared expenses across groups and calculates the minimum settlements needed to zero out all debts. It removes the mental overhead of tracking who paid what, so friend groups and roommates can focus on the experience rather than the accounting. Success looks like everyone agreeing on the numbers without a single argument.

## Brand Personality

Fun, clear, no-drama.

The mascot (a cheerful money bag) sets the tone: money doesn't have to be tense. The interface should feel like a friend who's good with numbers — helpful, light, never preachy or corporate.

## Anti-references

- Enterprise finance tools (QuickBooks, Xero) — sterile, form-heavy, built for accountants
- Overly serious fintech (dark navy, gold accents, trust-signal overkill) — Splitmate is for friends, not wealth management
- Feature-bloated social apps that bury the core task under notifications and feeds

## Design Principles

1. **No-drama clarity.** Every balance and settlement should be readable at a glance, without mental arithmetic. If the user has to think twice about a number, the UI failed.
2. **Friend-first tone.** Copy and affordances should feel like a helpful person, not a product. Avoid financial jargon; use plain language.
3. **Earn the trust quietly.** Playful doesn't mean careless. Numbers must be accurate and legible; color and motion should reinforce meaning, not distract from it.
4. **Minimum viable friction.** Logging an expense or settling a debt should take as few taps as possible. Every extra field or confirmation is a place the user gives up.
5. **Fairness on the surface.** Who owes whom should be visible without navigating. Balances are the product's core output — treat them as the hero, not a detail.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Color must not be the sole signal for financial states (positive/negative balances need more than green/red alone — use labels or signs). Adequate contrast across the neutral palette. No reduced-motion assumptions; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any animations added.
